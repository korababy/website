Kora Baby is not a medical app

Kora Baby is not a medical device. Kora Baby does not give medical advice. Kora Baby does not diagnose, treat, or monitor any condition, and it is not a substitute for your pediatrician.

What Kora Baby actually does is narrow, and it is worth being plain about it. It records what you tell it; shows you patterns, summaries and sleep estimates worked out from your own data; offers general guidance on starting solids and the weather where you are; and can remind you of things you asked to be reminded of. Nothing more.

· Kora Baby never tells you what a symptom means, never claims your baby has a condition, and never recommends a treatment, a remedy or a dose. Ordinary words like teething may appear in a food tip, in a shopping list, or in a note you wrote yourself. They are descriptions, never a claim about your baby.

· What Kora Baby shows you — a temperature you logged, your nights across a week, a symptom you recorded — is your own record laid out, so you have something concrete to mention at your next visit. It is an observation, never a finding, and Kora Baby never decides for you that something needs a doctor.

· The health report is built from what you logged. Some of it is arithmetic on those entries: weekly averages, and weight plotted against the WHO reference curves with a percentile. Those are conventional reference points, printed so a professional can read your log quickly. They are not an assessment of your baby, and Kora Baby draws no conclusion from them.

· When you log a medicine, Kora Baby stores the name and the dose exactly as you typed them. Kora Baby never checks a dose against anything — not your baby’s weight, not their age, not the medicine itself — even though it holds their weight. It will not warn you if a dose is wrong. Doses come from your pediatrician or the packaging. Never from Kora Baby.

· Growth entries can be shown against reference curves. That is a visual reference, not a judgment about how your baby is growing.

Anything about your baby’s health belongs with a professional — your pediatrician, doctor, midwife or nurse. Never delay getting medical advice, and never set aside advice you have already been given, because of something you saw in Kora Baby.

In an emergency, call your local emergency number or go to your nearest emergency department. Do not use Kora Baby.

Sleep times are estimates

Kora Baby shows when your baby may be ready to sleep next. With little or nothing logged, those times come from a chart of what is typical for a baby of that age — an average across many babies.

As you log sleep, Kora Baby blends in your baby’s own recent days — the last three weeks, the newest counting most — and leans on them more the more there are. The age chart never drops out completely.

Either way they are estimates. They can be half an hour out, or more, and they move as the day is logged. Kora Baby marks an expected time with “~” or the word “expected”, and Sleep’s accuracy card shows how far off they have been for your baby.

What is genuinely yours is everything the screens count back to you: the nights, the naps, the gaps between feeds, the growth line. Those are your own logs and nothing else.

None of it is clinical. The sleep estimates are worked out from your own baby’s logs, but they are not personalized medical advice. It is not a schedule to keep to and not a target to hit. Your baby is the authority on your baby. If the app and the child disagree, the child is right.

Reminders

Kora Baby can remind you of things — a feed or a pump interval, a visit tomorrow, and anything you name yourself, such as a medicine or a vitamin. Every reminder is off until you turn it on, each one can be turned off again in Settings, and you can set quiet hours.

These are worked out by your own phone, and all but the nap and bedtime reminders are rung by it alone. Those two can be sent by Kora Baby’s server instead, so that a sleep logged on another phone silences them — the privacy policy says how. They arrive silently. A lock-screen card for a running sleep is also different: it can be started from another parent’s phone, through Apple.

A reminder belongs to your baby: everyone in your family sees the same ones, with who added each, and each of you turns each one on or off for your own phone. One that someone else adds stays off on yours until you turn it on.

A reminder can be late, or can simply never arrive. A phone that is off, asleep or out of battery, a notification permission that was declined, a reminder that falls inside quiet hours, or a version of the app built before this feature existed — any of these means nothing appears. A reminder is a convenience, never an instruction, and never something to rely on for anything that matters. Medicines, visits and anything about your baby’s health need a real alarm or a real calendar, not Kora Baby.

Sharing a baby with family

A baby’s record has members. There are owners and there are caregivers. You can invite people with a code that lasts seven days and can be used once.

Inviting someone gives them the whole record. Every feed, every night, every diaper, every pumping session, every note, every photo — including diaper photos, rash photos, medicines and doses, growth measurements, visits and visit notes. Pumping entries are the mother’s own health information, and they are shared like everything else. Any member can also export the whole record, or generate the health report and take it off their phone. There is no partial view and no way to share only one part.

· A caregiver can add, edit and delete anything in that baby’s record.

· An owner can do all of that, and can also invite others and edit the baby’s profile. There is no way to delete a single baby’s record from inside the app — the only way to erase a record is to delete your account, which is described below.

· Everyone in a family can see the email addresses of the other members. If you join a family, your email address is visible to them.

Making someone an owner is not something you can take back. An owner cannot demote or remove another owner. Only that person can step down, or delete their own account. Make someone an owner only if you would trust them with the record permanently — that is what a co-parent is.

An owner can remove a caregiver. Anyone can leave a family themselves, except the last remaining owner, because a record has to belong to somebody.

What a member does with what they see is outside our control. Invite people you trust.
